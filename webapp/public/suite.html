Recruiter Psychology & Golden Rules

Recruiter-Side
Rule #1: Never State a Number First
Whoever speaks the first number anchors the conversation. If you anchor at $130K when their band max was $155K, you've permanently lost $25K before even starting. Always deflect back to their approved band.
Rule #2: The 24-Hour Silence Rule
When an offer is extended verbally, say: "Thank you so much! I am thrilled about this role and the team. Could you send the written compensation details over email so I can review the complete package and discuss it tomorrow?" Never negotiate while excited on the phone.
Rule #3: Total Package Arbitrage
If base salary is hard-capped by HR bands, pivot immediately to non-base levers: sign-on bonus ($10k-$25k), guaranteed 6-month performance review cycle, extra PTO, remote stipend, or accelerated equity vesting.
Rule #4: Competing Offer Framing
Never threaten. Frame competing offers as reluctance: "You are my clear #1 choice. However, I have another offer on the table at $148K with a $15K sign-on. If we can close the gap to $145K base, I am prepared to sign today and withdraw from other processes."

The 9-Phase Recruiter Due Diligence Protocol

Forensic Framework
PHASE 1

Digital Forensics

Domain age check (WHOIS), public ownership transparency, hosting location vs claimed corporate HQ, SSL validation (DV vs EV), corporate Secretary of State registration.

PHASE 2

Cross-Platform Footprint

LinkedIn company page employee headcount consistency, Glassdoor reviews, Indeed agency profile, BBB rating, Google Maps physical office Street View audit.

PHASE 3

Review Authenticity & Ghosting

Sentiment analysis, detection of clustered fake bot reviews, negative review spotlights, and the 3-channel Responsiveness Rating (Online, Offline, Recruiter On-Call).

PHASE 4

Business Model Audit

Deconstruct revenue engine: Employer-paid contingency/retained (legit) vs candidate training bundle fees vs third-party staffing body shop.

PHASE 5

Scam Signal Detection

Check for blacklists, ScamAdviser domain trust score, fake check scams, phishing patterns, early SSN/banking demands, and free email domains (@gmail/@outlook).

PHASE 6

Contradiction Engine ⚡

Compare what the firm claims against evidence: Claimed "500 placements" vs zero LinkedIn alumni; claimed "10 years" vs 6-month domain age; claimed "NYC HQ" vs mailbox rental.

PHASE 7

Verified Placement Proof

Search LinkedIn for genuine candidate announcements: "Thanks to [Firm] for helping me land my role at [Company]". Absence of placement proof is a strong credibility gap.

PHASE 8

Riskometer Scoring

Weighted matrix across Digital Trust (20%), Platform Presence (20%), Review Authenticity (20%), Business Model (20%), and Scam Signals (20%).

PHASE 9

Final Verdict

One-Line Brutal Truth, 3 Biggest Risks, Reasons It Might Still Be Legit, and actionable next steps for the candidate.

Top 4 Recruiter Traps & Scam Patterns

Threat Intelligence
TRAP #1: The Home Office Check Scam

Fake Equipment Advance

Recruiter sends an e-check or cashier check for $2,500–$5,000 to "buy computer monitors from our approved vendor." The check bounces 4 days later after you wire money to their fake vendor. Real companies ship pre-configured laptops directly via FedEx.

TRAP #2: The Third-Party Bench Placement Trap

OPT Visa & Wage Theft Trap

Staffing agency promises direct employment but places you on an unpaid "bench" while marketing your modified resume to third-party clients. Violates USCIS Form I-983 rules and creates critical immigration risk.

TRAP #3: The Upfront Placement / Training Fee

Pay-to-Play Scam

Agency claims you need a "$500 certification" or "mandatory onboarding background fee" before submitting your profile. Legitimate recruiters are paid 100% by the hiring employer upon successful placement. Candidates NEVER pay fees.

TRAP #4: The Resume Harvester / Bait-and-Switch

Phantom Job Postings

Agency posts glamorous Fortune 500 job postings with no actual contract. They collect hundreds of resumes to win vendor contracts or push candidates into lower-paying contract jobs. Always ask: "Is this an exclusive contract or active requisition?"
